# The 7 PM Quote Problem: Why Lawn Care Crews Lose Jobs After the Trucks Park

If you run a lawn care or landscaping business, you already know where the money leaks. It isn't on the job site. It's the three hours after.

A homeowner fills out your form at 6:42 PM. You're elbow-deep in payroll, your crew lead is texting about a busted mower, and dinner's getting cold. The quote doesn't go out tonight. It goes out tomorrow afternoon—maybe. By then, the homeowner already booked the competitor who replied at 7:05.

## Speed is the whole game

The data on this is brutal and consistent: the first contractor to respond wins the lion's share of home-service jobs. Not the cheapest. Not the best-reviewed. The fastest. Homeowners reaching out about their yard are usually contacting three or four companies in the same sitting, and most of them stop shopping the moment someone credible replies with a real number.

For a solo operator or a two-truck crew, "reply fast" is easier said than done. You can't quote at a stoplight. You can't price a fertilization program while you're edging a bed. The hours when leads come in—evenings and weekends—are exactly the hours you're least able to sit at a desk.

## The math nobody wants to do

Say you get 20 inbound leads a month. If even a quarter of them go cold because you replied too late, that's five jobs a month walking to a competitor. At an average ticket of a few hundred dollars for a one-time service—or a few thousand for a recurring maintenance contract—that's not a rounding error. That's a truck payment. That's a hire you can't make.

And it compounds. The recurring contract you lost in March isn't a March problem. It's a March-through-November problem, every cut, every visit, gone to the other guy.
