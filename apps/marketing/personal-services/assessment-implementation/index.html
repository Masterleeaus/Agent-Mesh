Personal Services · The working path

Understand first. Agree the work. Then implement.

The sequence below describes an engagement approach, not a delivery guarantee. Any work depends on an agreed scope, access, feasibility and written responsibilities.

Assessment to operation

Keep each decision in view.

Assessment should clarify what is useful, what needs attention and what must be checked before a change is proposed.

  1. 01

    Understand the service

    Describe appointments, service types, staff roles, customer touchpoints and constraints that shape the work.

  2. 02

    Map current systems

    List tools, information flows and existing processes. No named integration is assumed to be supported.

  3. 03

    Check fit and boundaries

    Review feasibility, third-party dependencies, data access, responsibilities and any required human review.

  4. 04

    Confirm a written scope

    Specify deliverables, commercial terms, responsibilities and intended timing before any implementation begins.

  5. 05

    Implement and review

    Work through the agreed changes and define how the result and any ongoing support will be reviewed.

Example discussion areas

Use cases to assess, not promises of automation.

Appointment-led businesses may choose to discuss workflows such as these. A discussion does not imply an integration, configured feature or service availability.

01

New service enquiry

Clarify what information a person needs before staff can respond or recommend a booking.

02

Appointment change

Map who reviews changes, how availability is checked and how the customer is updated.

03

Staff handover

Decide which service notes are useful, who may view them and how updates are kept current.

04

After-service follow-up

Define what follow-up belongs to the business and which steps, if any, are suitable to scope.
