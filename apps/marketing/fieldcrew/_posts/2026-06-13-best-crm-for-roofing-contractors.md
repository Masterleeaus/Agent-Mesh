Finding the best CRM for roofing contractors in 2026 is harder than it should be. The options range from lightweight job management tools to full-platform enterprise systems - and the right one depends almost entirely on your crew size, your sales volume, and how much you are willing to build out versus buy out of the box.

This comparison covers five CRMs that roofing contractors actually use: Jobber, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, AccuLynx, and HubSpot. For each one, the review is based on how contractors typically use these platforms - the workflows they support, the gaps they have, and the tradeoffs between them.

## What Roofing Contractors Actually Need From a CRM

Before comparing tools, it helps to define what "CRM" means for a roofing business - because the needs are different from a typical B2B sales operation.

A roofing CRM needs to handle:

- **Lead capture and tracking** - where did this lead come from, what did they say, who followed up
- **Estimate creation and status** - sent, viewed, accepted, declined
- **Job scheduling and crew assignment** - who is doing what and when
- **Follow-up automation** - leads that went quiet, estimates that were not answered
- **Invoice and payment** - tied to job completion
- **Review requests** - triggered after job close

Most roofing operations also deal with insurance claims, which adds a layer of documentation, adjuster communication, and supplement tracking that general CRMs do not handle well. That is where roofing-specific tools like AccuLynx pull ahead.

## Jobber: Best CRM for Roofing Contractors Running Small Crews

Jobber is built for home service businesses in the 1-15 employee range. When reviewing how roofing contractors use it, the platform handles the core job lifecycle cleanly: lead comes in, estimate goes out, job gets scheduled, invoice gets paid. The interface is straightforward enough that field crews can use it without training.

**What works well for roofers:**
- Job scheduling and crew dispatch
- Estimate templates that can be adjusted per job
- Automated appointment reminders and follow-up messages
- Client portal where homeowners can view and accept quotes
- Integration with QuickBooks for accounting

**Where it falls short:**
- No insurance claim workflow
- Limited sales pipeline customization compared to a dedicated CRM
- Reporting is basic - not ideal if you need detailed source attribution

**Best fit:** A roofing company doing residential repairs and replacements, with one or two estimators, that needs a clean tool for managing jobs from quote to invoice without a lot of setup.

## Housecall Pro: Best for Quick Setup and Consumer Experience

Housecall Pro targets the same crew size range as Jobber but leans harder into the consumer-facing experience. The online booking flow, automated review requests, and payment processing are polished. It also has a strong mobile app.

**What works well for roofers:**
- Online booking widget you can embed on your site
- Automated Google review requests after job completion
- Payment collection directly through the app
- Chat and two-way texting with customers built in
- Franchise and multi-location support

**Where it falls short:**
- Estimate customization is more limited than Jobber
- No insurance supplement workflow
- Less flexibility in automation sequences than some alternatives

**Best fit:** A roofing company that prioritizes a clean customer experience and wants automated reviews and payments with minimal configuration. Works especially well for companies that do a high volume of smaller repair jobs.

## ServiceTitan: Best CRM for Roofing Contractors Scaling to Enterprise

ServiceTitan is the enterprise option. It is used by large roofing companies running multiple crews, multiple markets, or operating across trades. The platform covers the full job lifecycle with detailed reporting, advanced dispatching, and marketing attribution. It also has the highest implementation complexity and cost.

**What works well for large roofing operations:**
- Full revenue reporting by source, tech, job type
- Advanced dispatch and routing
- Marketing ROI tracking down to the campaign level
- Financing integration for higher-ticket jobs
- Multi-trade support (relevant for companies that also do HVAC or gutters)

**Where it falls short:**
- Implementation takes weeks and usually requires outside help
- Monthly cost is significantly higher than alternatives
- Overkill for a roofing company doing under $2-3M in revenue

**Best fit:** A roofing company doing $3M or more annually with a dedicated office team, multiple estimators, and a need for detailed business reporting. Also a fit for multi-trade companies that want a single platform.

## AccuLynx: Best CRM for Roofing Contractors Working Insurance Jobs

AccuLynx is built specifically for roofing. That specificity is its main advantage. The platform handles the workflows that general job management tools miss: tracking insurance claims, storing adjuster communications, managing supplement requests, and moving a job through the insurance process.

**What works well:**
- Insurance claim workflow with status tracking
- Document storage for adjuster reports, photos, and correspondence
- Supplement management and approval tracking
- Material ordering integrated into the job workflow
- Crew-facing tools for photo documentation on-site

**Where it falls short:**
- Less polished on the general CRM and sales pipeline side
- Higher learning curve for the insurance modules
- Not ideal if most of your work is retail (non-insurance) jobs

**Best fit:** A roofing company where insurance restoration is a significant portion of revenue. If you regularly work with adjusters, submit supplements, and track claim timelines, AccuLynx is built for that workflow in a way that no general platform is.

## HubSpot: Best CRM for Roofing Contractors With High Lead Volume

HubSpot is not a field service tool - it is a sales CRM. But for roofing companies with active outbound sales teams, high lead volume from multiple sources, or a commercial sales operation, it handles the front-end of the funnel better than any of the above.

**What works well for sales-heavy roofing operations:**
- Free tier is genuinely functional for small teams
- Pipeline tracking is flexible and visual
- Email sequences for lead follow-up can be automated
- Reporting on lead source, stage, and conversion rate
- Integration with marketing tools (forms, ads, email campaigns)

**Where it falls short:**
- No job scheduling or field dispatch
- No estimate workflow
- No invoice or payment tools
- Not built for managing jobs once they are sold

**Best fit:** A roofing company with an inside sales team, heavy inbound lead flow, or commercial sales effort - typically paired with a separate job management tool for the operations side. Also a good option for smaller companies that want to start free and upgrade over time.

## Questions to Ask Before You Commit to a Roofing CRM

Before signing up for a free trial or requesting a demo, get clear on these questions. The answers will narrow the field faster than any feature comparison.

**What is the primary problem you are trying to solve?** If it is "leads fall through the cracks," you need a CRM with follow-up automation. If it is "we spend too much time on paperwork," you need estimate and job management. If it is "insurance jobs take too long to process," you need roofing-specific workflow tools. The problem you start with determines the platform category.

**How many people will use the system?** A solo roofer plus an office manager can run on Jobber's entry tier. A company with three estimators, a dispatch team, and a field crew needs something that handles multi-user permissions and concurrent workflows without getting messy.

**Do you need it to work offline?** Field crews on jobs often work in areas with poor cell coverage. If your estimators need to pull up job details, take photos, or create quotes in the field without a signal, check whether the mobile app has offline functionality before you commit.

**What does your current software stack look like?** If you are already using QuickBooks, check which CRMs integrate directly. If you use Google Workspace for scheduling, check whether the CRM syncs with Google Calendar. Switching is easier when the new tool connects to what you already have rather than replacing everything.

**What does onboarding actually cost?** The advertised monthly price for ServiceTitan rarely reflects the real first-year cost. Ask each vendor directly: what is included in onboarding, is there an implementation fee, and how long before you are fully set up? This question alone eliminates surprises.

**Can you export your data if you leave?** Switching CRMs after a year is painful but sometimes necessary. Before you start, confirm that you can export your contact list, job history, and documents in a standard format. Vendors that make export difficult are building a lock-in moat - that is worth knowing upfront.

## How to Choose the Best CRM for Your Roofing Business

Based on reviewing how roofing contractors use these platforms, here is a practical decision framework:

**If you are under $1M in annual revenue:** Start with Jobber or Housecall Pro. Both are affordable, fast to set up, and cover the core workflow. Do not over-invest in a platform before you have the volume to justify it.

**If insurance restoration is your primary business model:** Look hard at AccuLynx. The insurance-specific workflow saves significant administrative time and reduces missed supplements.

**If you are scaling past $3M and need business reporting:** ServiceTitan becomes worth the investment. Below that revenue threshold, the complexity-to-value ratio is usually unfavorable.

**If you have a dedicated sales team or high inbound lead volume:** Consider HubSpot for the CRM layer and pair it with a simpler job management tool for operations.

**If your biggest problem is lead follow-up, not job management:** Any of these tools can be connected to automation that handles the follow-up gap. The CRM is the tracking layer; the automation is what sends the texts and emails when a lead goes quiet.

According to [Salesforce research on response time](https://www.salesforce.com/resources/articles/customer-expectations/), companies that respond to leads within an hour are far more likely to qualify them than those that wait longer. The CRM is only as valuable as the process built around it.

## What to Set Up First After Choosing a Roofing CRM

Most contractors who invest in a CRM see limited results not because the platform is wrong but because they only use it passively - logging jobs, creating invoices, and nothing else. The contractors who see meaningful ROI from a CRM are the ones who build active workflows on top of the contact tracking.

**In the first week:** Import all existing contacts and open leads. Set up your standard estimate template with your most common job types, material specs, and labor rates. Get your whole team using the mobile app for job updates. An unused CRM is just expensive spreadsheet software.

**In the first month:** Build your follow-up sequence for estimates that go unanswered. Most platforms support automated reminders - set a message to go out three days after an estimate is sent and again at seven days if there is no response. This single workflow recovers a meaningful percentage of leads that would otherwise go cold.

**Set up automated review requests.** After every job is marked complete, the CRM should trigger a review request via text or email. Timing matters - send it within 24 to 48 hours of project completion, while the homeowner's experience is fresh. Most CRMs support this natively or through a connected tool.

**Build a source-tracking habit.** Train everyone on the team to log where each lead came from when they enter it. Within three months, you will have real data on which channels are producing jobs - Google ads, yard signs, referrals, online search, or something else. That data is more valuable than any marketing assumption.

A CRM assessment is worth doing before you commit to a platform. Understanding what your current workflow looks like - where leads enter, how they are tracked, where they fall off - shapes which tool will actually solve your problem versus add complexity.

The most common failure when reviewing contractor operations is not choosing the wrong CRM - it is choosing any CRM and then not building the follow-up and automation that makes it work. A well-configured Jobber account will outperform a poorly configured ServiceTitan account every time.

A CRM tracks data. It does not automatically follow up with leads that went quiet, send review requests after job completion, or re-engage estimates that were not answered. Those workflows need to be built, either inside the platform or through connected tools.

The difference between a contractor with a CRM and a contractor whose CRM actually generates revenue is usually the automation layer built on top of the tracking layer.
