14 Assessments, 11 Quotes Drafted, ~$38K in Pipeline: How One Lawn Crew Stopped Leaving Money on the Table

JG
Justin Grant
Jun 24, 2026 · 4 min read

It's easy to talk about "AI for your back office" in the abstract. It's more useful to show what it actually did for a real lawn care operation. So here's a real one.

The setup

Firsthand Lawns is a growing landscaping operation that runs into the same wall every expanding crew hits: more leads than hours to handle them. The owner was doing the work and running the back office—assessing properties during the day, then trying to turn those assessments into sent quotes at night. Some quotes went out fast. Plenty didn't go out at all.

That's the quiet killer in this business. It's not that you lose deals. It's that you never get around to making the offer in the first place. The assessment happens, the day rolls on, and three of those properties never receive a number.

What we ran

We pointed Drafted at the inbound assessments and let the Quote Drafter do what it does: pull each assessment, apply the pricing logic, and generate a ready-to-send quote overnight. No new CRM. No process overhaul. The leads came in the way they always had—the difference was what happened to them after dark.

The result

Out of 14 property assessments, Drafted turned around 11 drafted quotes—each one waiting for review the next morning instead of sitting as a half-finished thought. Those quotes represented roughly $38,000 in pipeline: real, quotable, sendable revenue that, in the old workflow, would have leaked out in dribs and drabs as "I'll get to it tomorrow."

That's an 11-out-of-14 conversion from assessment to actual drafted offer. The bottleneck was never the leads. It was the gap between assessing a property and getting a real number in front of the homeowner—and that gap is exactly what closed.

Why this is the part that matters

Pipeline isn't closed revenue. Some of those 11 quotes will land, some won't—that's sales. But you can't win a job you never quoted. The entire game is getting a credible number in front of the homeowner before they hire someone else, and getting it in front of all of them, not just the three you had energy for that night.

Eleven quotes drafted overnight means eleven homeowners got a real response fast. The owner spent the morning reviewing and sending, not building eleven estimates from scratch. The hours that used to disappear into back-office catch-up went back into the business.

The takeaway for your crew

If you're assessing properties and not converting most of them into sent quotes, the problem usually isn't your pricing or your pitch. It's throughput—the simple fact that there aren't enough night-time hours to turn every assessment into an offer.
