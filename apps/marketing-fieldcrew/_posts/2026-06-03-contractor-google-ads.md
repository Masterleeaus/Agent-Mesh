If you have ever run contractor Google Ads and watched your budget drain away without the phone ringing, you are not alone. Most home services businesses that try paid search overspend and underperform - not because the platform does not work, but because the setup is wrong from day one. [According to WordStream's home services industry benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2016/02/29/google-adwords-industry-benchmarks), average conversion rates in the home services vertical sit around 7-8%, and plenty of accounts are doing far worse than that.

The gap between a campaign that breaks even and one that delivers a consistent cost per lead is almost never about budget size. It is about campaign structure, targeting discipline, and what happens after the click. This post breaks down exactly where most contractor campaigns fall apart and gives you a concrete path to fix it.

## Why Most Contractor Google Ads Campaigns Waste Budget

The most common pattern in underperforming contractor ad accounts: the campaign is running, clicks are coming in, budget is being spent - but leads are thin. Pull the search terms report and the problem becomes clear immediately.

A campaign bidding on broad match keywords like "AC repair" ends up serving ads for searches like "DIY AC repair YouTube," "AC repair technician school near me," and "how to fix AC for free." Every one of those clicks costs money. None of them will ever produce a paying customer.

This is the match type problem, and it is the single biggest budget drain in home services campaigns. But it rarely shows up alone. Other culprits appear consistently when reviewing underperforming accounts:

- **No negative keyword list.** Without actively excluding irrelevant searches, Google keeps serving your ads for terms that have nothing to do with paying work. "Free," "DIY," "how to," "school," "certification," and "jobs" alone can drain a significant chunk of a small monthly budget.
- **No call tracking.** If you cannot see which keywords and ads are generating phone calls, you have no way to know what is working. You might be pausing your best keyword and scaling your worst one.
- **No conversion tracking at all.** [Google Ads relies on conversion data](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6365) to optimize Smart Bidding strategies. Without it, the platform is guessing, and you are paying for its guesses.
- **A generic homepage as the landing page destination.** This gets its own section below because it deserves it.

Most contractors who set up Google Ads themselves - or who hire a generalist marketing agency - make two or three of these mistakes simultaneously, producing a campaign that technically runs but reliably fails to deliver a workable cost per lead.

## The Match Type Problem That Is Eating Your Ad Spend

The keyword match type you choose determines how closely a search must match your keyword before your ad shows.

**Broad match** shows your ad for searches loosely related to your keyword, including synonyms and conceptually connected queries. For "HVAC contractor," that means ads for "HVAC job openings" or "commercial HVAC parts supplier" - and you pay for every click regardless of intent.

**Phrase match** shows your ad for searches that include your keyword's meaning - more flexibility than exact match, less than broad. It has tightened in recent years but still leaves room for drift in competitive, noisy markets.

**Exact match** shows your ad when the search query closely matches your keyword. This is the most controlled option and typically delivers the highest-intent traffic.

For most contractors under $3,000/month, start with a combination of phrase match and exact match. Use exact match on your highest-priority terms - "emergency plumber [city]," "AC repair [city]," "licensed electrician [city]" - and phrase match to capture intent-aligned variations you might not have anticipated.

Commit to reviewing your search terms report weekly for the first 60 days. Add every irrelevant term to your negative keyword list immediately and keep building it. A well-maintained list can reduce wasted spend by 20-40% without changing bids, budgets, or ad copy.

Start your negative keyword list with these categories on day one:
- Informational intent: DIY, how to, tutorial, guide, YouTube
- Price-avoidance signals: free, cheap, cheapest, lowest price, no cost
- Career and trade education: jobs, careers, training, school, apprenticeship, certification, license exam
- Supplier or wholesale intent: parts only, wholesale, supplier, distributor
- Competitor brand names you are not targeting intentionally

## Your Landing Page Is the Silent Killer

You can build a technically solid campaign - right match types, clean negative keyword list, well-written ads - and still hemorrhage money if the page people land on is wrong.

The most common mistake is routing all clicks to the homepage. Homepages are built for browsing, not converting - navigation menus, competing links, general brand messaging, no focused call to action. A prospective customer who clicks an ad for "emergency furnace repair" and lands on a page about your 30 years of experience and full menu of services will leave without acting.

A dedicated landing page mirrors the intent of the search. If someone searched "emergency furnace repair Richmond VA," the headline should say "Emergency Furnace Repair in Richmond - We Answer 24/7." No navigation links to distract them, one primary call to action, and enough trust signals to make them comfortable calling.

A converting home services landing page needs:

- **A headline that directly matches the search intent.** Page-level relevance is the single biggest driver of conversion rate.
- **A phone number visible above the fold.** Click-to-call is the fastest conversion path on mobile, where the majority of home services searches happen.
- **Social proof near the top.** Star rating, review count, and one short testimonial from a real customer reduce hesitation faster than any amount of descriptive copy.
- **Service area confirmation.** "Serving [City] and surrounding areas" tells the searcher immediately that you are available to them.
- **A fast load time.** Every additional second of load time costs you leads, particularly on mobile.
- **One clear call to action.** Not four options - one. Call now, or book online. Make it impossible to miss.

If you run multiple service lines, build a separate landing page for each campaign. An electrician advertising both panel upgrades and EV charger installation needs two pages, each speaking directly to that specific search intent.

This single change - from homepage to dedicated landing page - is often the highest-leverage fix in an underperforming contractor campaign.

## How to Structure a Contractor Google Ads Campaign That Actually Converts

Here is a practical, step-by-step framework for building a Search campaign for a home services business. This applies whether you are starting from scratch or rebuilding a campaign that has not been working.

1. **Map your campaign structure before opening Google Ads.**

Separate campaigns by service line, not geography. An HVAC company should have separate campaigns for AC repair, furnace repair, AC installation, and seasonal tune-ups. This structure lets you control budget and bids at the service level and makes performance data readable when you need to make decisions.

2. **Build tight, intent-focused keyword lists for each campaign.**

For each campaign, develop a list of 15-30 keywords that represent genuine buying intent for that specific service. Focus on terms that include action words - "repair," "install," "replace," "fix," "emergency" - combined with local modifiers like your city name, county name, or "near me." Avoid high-volume informational keywords that attract researchers, not buyers.

Use the [Google Ads Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) to estimate search volume and CPCs in your market. Home services CPCs range widely - roughly $6 for low-competition maintenance searches up to $30 or more for high-value installation keywords in dense urban markets.

3. **Write ad copy that speaks directly to the search intent.**

Your primary headline should contain the target keyword. Your second headline should include either a local qualifier or a value differentiator - "Licensed and Insured," "Same-Day Service Available," "Serving [City] Since 2008." Description lines should focus on what happens next: "Call now for a free estimate" or "Book online in under 60 seconds."

Write at least three headline variants per ad group. Google's Responsive Search Ads format allows up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, testing combinations automatically and surfacing top performers.

4. **Set up call tracking before the campaign goes live.**

You cannot optimize a campaign you cannot measure. Set up Google's native call extensions and call conversion tracking, or integrate a third-party tool like CallRail for more granular attribution. Track "calls of 60 seconds or longer" as your primary conversion event - short calls are frequently wrong numbers or spam, so filtering by duration gives you cleaner data to optimize against.

If you receive form submissions, set those up as secondary conversion events. For most home services businesses, phone calls are where revenue starts and deserve the most precise tracking you can apply.

5. **Route every click to a dedicated landing page.**

As covered in the previous section - no homepages. Each campaign gets its own landing page. The headline on that page should reflect the search intent of the keywords in that campaign. The call to action should be singular and prominent.

6. **Start with manual or Enhanced CPC bidding, then move to Smart Bidding once you have data.**

Smart Bidding strategies like Target CPA require conversion history to function - a brand-new campaign has none. Generate at least 30-50 conversions over the first 60-90 days, then test a Smart Bidding strategy. Launching a cold account on Target CPA from day one typically results in underdelivery, erratic spend, or inflated costs per lead.

7. **Review search terms weekly and continuously refine your negative keyword list.**

Review your search terms report weekly for the first three months. Add every irrelevant term to your negative keyword list. Identify high-performing search queries and add them as exact match keywords in their own tightly focused ad groups. This ongoing refinement is what separates campaigns that compound value over time from those that plateau at mediocre performance.

## Local Services Ads: A Smarter Complement to Your Search Campaign

Google Local Services Ads (LSAs) deserve separate attention because they operate differently from traditional Search campaigns - and for many contractors, they provide a lower-cost path to qualified leads.

LSAs appear above traditional paid search ads at the very top of the results page. They display your business name, star rating, review count, years in business, and a "Google Guaranteed" badge for verified and eligible businesses. Critically, you pay per lead rather than per click, which eliminates a major category of wasted spend.

The tradeoffs are real: LSAs require background checks and license verification through Google, and your ranking within the LSA block is influenced heavily by review volume and recency - not just bid amount. In competitive markets, a business with 300 reviews will consistently outrank one with 50, regardless of budget. If your review count is low, build it up before leaning heavily on LSAs.

For most home services contractors, running both channels simultaneously makes sense. LSAs capture top-of-page real estate with a pay-per-lead cost structure. Search campaigns give you tighter control over which services you promote, what your messaging says, and which landing pages your traffic hits.

If you need to pick one: strong review count points toward LSAs first, where cost per lead tends to be more predictable. If you need more message control or are targeting specific high-margin services, a properly structured Search campaign gives more flexibility.

## The Post-Click Gap: Where Contractor Campaigns Lose the Most Revenue

Here is what most campaign managers do not talk about: the click is not where most campaigns actually fail. It is what happens after.

A prospective customer finds your ad, clicks through to your landing page, and either calls your number or submits a form. What happens next determines whether that lead becomes revenue or a wasted ad dollar.

Research consistently shows that leads contacted within five minutes of first inquiry are dramatically more likely to convert than those reached after 30 minutes. After an hour, a home services lead has almost certainly already called someone else.

Reviewing contractor ad accounts alongside CRM data and call logs, the same pattern emerges: the campaign is generating leads at a reasonable cost per lead, but the close rate is low. The cause is almost always slow response time and inconsistent follow-up.

The math on this is direct. A campaign generating 40 leads per month at $75 per lead has a total ad spend of $3,000. If your team closes 15 of those leads at an average ticket of $1,200, you have spent $3,000 to generate $18,000 in revenue - a strong return. If you only close 5 because leads are sitting unanswered for hours or getting a single call attempt before being abandoned, you are spending the same $3,000 to generate $6,000. The ad campaign looks like it is not working. What is actually not working is the follow-up.

What this means practically for your operation:

- **Calls need to be answered.** If you are spending real money on ads, you need a reliable system for capturing calls when your technicians are on jobs. That could mean a dedicated intake person, a professional answering service, or a callback system that triggers the moment a call is missed.
- **Missed calls need an immediate text response.** A lead who hits voicemail will often move on before you call back - unless they receive an automatic text within 60 seconds acknowledging their call. [How Missed Call Text-Back Works for Contractors](/blog/missed-call-text-back-contractor/) covers the mechanics of setting this up.
- **Form leads need a multi-step follow-up sequence, not a single call attempt.** Most leads require multiple contact points before connecting. An automated sequence combining text and email over 48-72 hours dramatically improves contact rates. [How to Automate Follow-Ups With Leads Who Don't Call Back](/blog/contractor-lead-follow-up-automation/) walks through how to build this for a field service business.
- **All leads need to land in a CRM.** If your leads are scattered across a Gmail inbox, a Google Ads dashboard, and a spreadsheet, you have no visibility into which campaigns are actually producing closed jobs versus clicks. A CRM that logs calls, tracks lead status, and triggers follow-up tasks is not optional when you are spending serious money on paid traffic.

The ROI on fixing your post-click process is often higher than improving your campaign structure. Both matter - but if your follow-up is broken, better match types and landing pages just mean more leads falling through the same holes faster.

## Contractor Google Ads FAQ

**How much should a contractor budget for Google Ads?**

Most home services contractors need a minimum of $1,500-$2,000/month to generate enough data to optimize effectively and enough lead volume to keep a schedule reasonably full. In competitive metro markets, HVAC and plumbing businesses regularly spend $3,000-$6,000+ per month to capture a meaningful share of local search volume. Plan to sustain your budget for at least 90 days - pulling the plug at 30 days, before the campaign has enough conversion history to optimize, is one of the most common reasons contractors conclude Google Ads doesn't work.

**What is a realistic cost per lead for home services Google Ads?**

Cost per lead varies significantly by service type, market size, and campaign quality. WordStream's home services data puts average CPLs in a range of roughly $33-$75, but that average covers a wide spread. Emergency services - burst pipe, no heat in winter - tend to command higher CPCs and attract urgent buyers who convert at higher rates. Installation and replacement campaigns are expensive and competitive but carry high average ticket values that support the cost. Maintenance and tune-up campaigns produce cheaper leads but lower revenue per job. Knowing your average ticket by service type is essential for understanding what a lead is actually worth.

**Should a contractor use broad match keywords?**

In most cases, no - at least not with a limited budget on a new campaign. Broad match gives Google wide latitude, and the platform's definition of "related" often surprises advertisers. Once you have a strong negative keyword list and 90+ days of conversion history, testing a small set of broad match keywords in a controlled campaign can be worth trying. Start with phrase match and exact match as your default.

**How long does it take for Google Ads to start working for a contractor?**

Plan for three to six months before a campaign reaches full optimization. The first 30-60 days are about generating enough conversions to make data-driven decisions. Months two and three refine match types, negative keywords, ad copy, and landing pages based on actual performance. Smart Bidding strategies typically need 60-90 days of consistent conversion history to stabilize. Contractors who stop at 45 days are often quitting right before the campaign would have turned a corner.

**Is Google Ads worth it for a smaller contractor with a tight budget?**

It can be, but the margin for error is smaller. A $500 monthly budget in a competitive local market often will not generate enough click volume to optimize effectively. If budget is limited, Local Services Ads may be a better entry point - you pay per lead rather than per click, and the risk of wasted spend is lower. Combining a modest paid budget with consistent organic search investment is another option. [Google Ads vs. SEO for Contractors](/blog/google-ads-vs-seo-contractors/) lays out how both channels compare for home services businesses at different stages.

**What is the biggest mistake contractors make with Google Ads?**

The single most common and costly mistake, seen across HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and landscaping accounts, is running broad match keywords without a negative keyword list while sending traffic to a homepage. This combination guarantees wasted spend. Fix the match types, build the negative keyword list, and create dedicated landing pages - in that order - and most underperforming campaigns improve meaningfully within 60 days.
