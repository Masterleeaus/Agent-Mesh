If you run an HVAC, plumbing, or any residential field service business, you have probably noticed the cluster of ads above every other result when someone searches "plumber near me" or "AC repair [city name]." Those are Local Service Ads -- they claim the very top of Google, above the map pack, organic results, and standard pay-per-click campaigns. They work differently from other Google advertising, and for the right contractor, they can be one of the most cost-effective lead sources in 2026.

This guide covers how Local Service Ads (LSAs) work, what the verification process actually requires, how bidding and ranking are determined, and what needs to happen on your end after a lead comes in to make the economics work.

## What Local Service Ads for Contractors Actually Are

Local Service Ads are Google's pay-per-lead product built for service businesses. Unlike traditional Google Ads, where you pay for every click regardless of outcome, LSAs charge you only when a verified lead contacts you -- by calling, messaging, or booking through the ad.

The ads appear at the very top of the search results page, showing two to three businesses with a name, star rating, review count, years in business, and a "Google Guaranteed" or "Google Screened" badge. Clicking a listing opens a profile page where customers can call or message directly.

**Key differences from standard pay-per-click advertising:**

- **Pay per lead, not per click.** You are charged when someone actually contacts you through the ad, not when they browse past it.
- **Google Guaranteed badge.** Google backs jobs booked through your ad with a satisfaction guarantee for the homeowner (up to $2,000 for most home service categories). You complete the verification; Google covers the guarantee.
- **No keyword lists.** You set a weekly budget and a max cost-per-lead, and Google matches your ad to relevant searches in your defined service area. There are no individual keywords to manage or bid on.
- **Verification requirements.** Your business, your licenses, and your field technicians must all pass background and credential checks before your ads go live. This barrier is what makes the Google Guaranteed badge meaningful.

LSAs target residential, high-intent demand -- someone with a problem right now looking for a contractor today. That is what makes them so valuable for HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and similar trades.

## Google Guaranteed vs. Google Screened: What the Badge Actually Means

Two types of badges appear on LSA profiles, and they apply to different business types. Understanding the difference matters because it affects what Google checks -- and what homeowners are promised.

**Google Guaranteed** applies to home service businesses: HVAC contractors, plumbers, electricians, landscapers, roofers, and similar trades. To earn the badge, your business must pass:

1. A background check on the business owner
2. Background checks on any field technicians who work inside customer homes
3. License verification for your trade category (the specific license requirements vary by state and job type)
4. Insurance verification -- at minimum, a current general liability certificate naming the business

Once verified, homeowners who book through your LSA are covered by Google's guarantee -- up to $2,000 (lifetime cap per customer) if they are not satisfied with the work. The guarantee applies only to jobs booked through the ad, not other channels.

**Google Screened** applies to professional services like attorneys, financial planners, and real estate agents. The verification process is similar, but the consumer-facing guarantee works differently. [Google's Google Screened documentation](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9018876) covers the specifics for professional service categories.

For contractors in the trades, Google Guaranteed is the relevant program.

**How verification actually works:**

Background checks are run by Evident, Google's third-party screening partner. After you submit your application, the business owner and each field employee receives an email from Evident to complete screening, typically within three to seven business days.

License and insurance review is handled manually by Google. Upload your documentation in the LSA dashboard. The most common delays come from mismatched business names (license vs. Google account), expired certificates, or licenses that do not match the job categories selected.

The badge matters beyond a visual signal. Verified businesses consistently close inbound leads at higher rates than unverified competitors -- the trust is built into the first impression, before a homeowner ever speaks to your team.

## How Local Service Ads Bidding and Ranking Work for Contractors

LSAs do not use the keyword auction model that powers standard Google Ads. Instead, Google uses a combination of factors to decide which contractors appear at the top of the LSA section and in what order:

**Your max cost-per-lead bid.** You set the maximum you are willing to pay per verified lead. Higher bids increase visibility, but bidding high alone does not guarantee the top position.

**Your proximity to the searcher.** Google heavily weights geography. A contractor five miles from the searcher will typically outrank one thirty miles away. Define your service area realistically -- an overly large radius dilutes your proximity advantage in your core market.

**Your review score and review volume.** A business with 4.8 stars and 90 reviews will generally outrank a 5.0-star business with 6 reviews. Both rating and count factor in -- and this is one of the most durable competitive advantages you can build over time.

**Your responsiveness rate.** Google tracks what percentage of leads you respond to and how quickly. Missed calls, ignored messages, and unacknowledged leads push your ranking down. This is one of the most significant ranking variables -- and entirely within your control.

**Your weekly budget.** If you exhaust your budget early in the week, your ads stop showing until the following week. Set a realistic budget for your market size and review spend on a weekly cadence, especially in seasonal peaks.

**Your dispute and chargeback history.** Disputing leads in good faith is normal. Disputing at an unusually high rate, or disputing clearly legitimate leads, will negatively affect your account.

There is no published formula for how these factors are weighted, but the pattern is consistent: contractors who respond quickly, maintain strong review profiles, and bid competitively rank at the top -- regardless of company size. A well-run three-truck operation can outrank a large franchise if the fundamentals are in order.

## How to Set Up Local Service Ads for Your Contracting Business

The LSA setup process takes longer than launching a standard Google Ads campaign because of the verification requirements. Here is the complete process from start to live:

1. **Confirm eligibility and create your account.**
Go to [ads.google.com/local-services-ads](https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads) and enter your business type and zip code. If your category is active in your market, you will be prompted to create an account. Sign in with a Google account tied to your business email (not a personal Gmail if you can avoid it -- keeping your advertising accounts tied to a business email simplifies access management if your team grows).

2. **Complete your business profile.**
Fill out your business name, service area, hours, and job types. Set your service area by zip code list, city, or radius -- be deliberate. Overly broad areas dilute your proximity advantage in the neighborhoods where you do most of your work. Start with your core geography and expand later.

3. **Select your job types carefully.**
LSAs match your ads to searches based on the job types you select -- not keywords. Only select categories you actually handle. Irrelevant leads from wrongly selected categories are harder to dispute and waste your budget.

4. **Submit your license and insurance documentation.**
Upload your contractor's license and a current general liability certificate. Both must be valid and name the same business entity as your Google account. A DBA/license name mismatch is the most common stall point -- if this applies, upload both the DBA registration and underlying entity documents.

5. **Complete background checks for all field employees.**
Once submitted, Evident emails the business owner and each added technician to complete screening. Everyone who enters customer homes -- including seasonal employees and subcontractors working under your brand -- must be screened. Plan for three to seven business days per person.

6. **Set your budget, review your profile, and go live.**
Once approved, set your weekly budget and max cost-per-lead. Google suggests ranges based on your market -- treat these as starting points, not commitments. Start conservatively and increase after a few weeks of lead data. Before going live, review your profile as a customer would: confirm your description, hours, and reviews look right. Ads typically start showing within 24 hours of approval.

7. **Connect your lead handling workflow before your first lead arrives.**
Every lead needs to be answered, logged, and followed up with. Configure call forwarding so calls route correctly, and enable push notifications for message leads. If you use a CRM, set up the integration now so leads flow in automatically rather than being logged manually.

## How to Dispute Invalid Leads and Get Credits Back

One of the most valuable LSA features -- and the most underused -- is the lead dispute process. Google allows you to dispute specific categories of bad leads and receive a credit against future charges.

**Leads you can dispute:**

- Calls from people located outside your defined service area
- Calls for job types you do not offer (if you set up your categories correctly, this should be rare)
- Duplicate leads from the same person about the same job
- Calls that lasted under 30 seconds (clearly not a genuine inquiry)
- Automated calls, robocalls, or spam

**How to submit a dispute:**
In your LSA dashboard, go to the Leads section, find the lead, and select "Dispute." Choose a reason from the dropdown. Google reviews disputes manually and typically responds within three to five business days. Approved disputes come back as credits against future charges, not cash.

**What is not disputable:**
A homeowner who called, asked real questions, and hired a competitor is a legitimate lead. You paid for the opportunity to compete, not the job itself. Disputing losses will hurt your account standing and will be denied.

Review your leads weekly and dispute anything that clearly qualifies. Contractors who dispute consistently recover 5 to 15 percent of their monthly spend from spam, out-of-area, and zero-duration calls -- but only if someone is checking the dashboard regularly.

## What Happens After the Lead Comes In

Running LSAs is only half the equation. What your business does in the minutes after a lead arrives is where most of the variability in ROI actually lives.

**Speed to answer is the single biggest lever.** Responding within five minutes produces dramatically higher booking rates than waiting an hour or more. Calls need to be answered by a live person during business hours -- not voicemail, not an answering service that cannot book. A missed call means you paid for a lead you did not convert.

**Set up a missed-call text-back system for overflow and after-hours.**
Even the best-staffed shop misses calls. An automated text triggered when a call goes unanswered keeps the lead in the conversation -- something like "Thanks for calling [Business Name] -- we are on another call but will get back to you within the hour" dramatically reduces lead bleed. See [How Missed Call Text-Back Works for Contractors](/blog/missed-call-text-back-contractor/) for setup guidance.

**Log every LSA lead in your CRM.**
Without a CRM, you can track cost per lead but not cost per booked job, conversion by job type, or whether the ad spend is actually working. A basic setup does not need to be complicated. See [CRM for Contractors: What to Use and How to Set It Up](/blog/crm-for-contractors/) for practical options at every business size.

**Build a follow-up sequence for leads who did not book on the first call.**
A homeowner who said "I need to think about it" is not a lost lead -- they are a warm prospect. Without follow-up, they will book with whoever calls next. A two-touch sequence -- a call the following day, a text two days later -- recovers a meaningful share of these prospects. See [How to Automate Follow-Ups With Leads Who Don't Call Back](/blog/contractor-lead-follow-up-automation/) for a practical setup.

**Actively request reviews from every customer who books through LSA.**
Build review requests into your job completion workflow -- a text after the tech leaves, a follow-up email the next morning. Your review count and rating directly affect your LSA ranking, and every completed job is an opportunity to improve both.

Contractors who treat every inbound lead as a priority -- whether it arrives at 8 a.m. or 7:30 p.m. -- generate significantly more booked jobs per dollar of ad spend than those who respond when convenient. LSAs amplify whatever your lead handling process looks like. If it is tight, LSAs work. If it is loose, LSAs become an expensive lesson.

## Common Local Service Ads Mistakes Contractors Make

Setting up LSAs correctly is straightforward, but several common mistakes cause contractors to waste budget or underperform in rankings.

**Not completing verification before going live.** Running ads before your background check and license verification are confirmed means your ads show without the Google Guaranteed badge. The badge is the primary trust signal that separates LSA from regular search ads. Pause until verification clears.

**Setting your service area too broadly.** More geographic coverage sounds like more leads, but LSA ranking considers your proximity to the searcher. A broad area that includes cities where you rarely work dilutes your relevance signal. Set your service area to where you actually want to dispatch crews.

**Ignoring your review count.** LSA ranking weights your Google Business Profile reviews. Contractors with 10 reviews will lose position to contractors with 50 reviews in the same category, even with equal bids. If you are not requesting reviews systematically after every completed job, your LSA spend is working harder than it needs to.

**Letting unqualified leads sit.** The dispute window is typically 30 days. If you do not log in regularly and dispute leads that do not meet the criteria, you forfeit those credits. Review your lead history weekly.

**Not connecting your LSA profile to your Google Business Profile.** LSAs pull your reviews from your GBP. If they are not linked, your review count does not carry over, which directly affects your ranking. Complete the linking step during setup and verify it is active.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Local Service Ads for Contractors

**How much do Local Service Ads cost per lead?**

Cost per lead varies by category and market. Plumbing and HVAC leads in major metros typically run $25 to $75. Electrical averages $20 to $60. Landscaping and cleaning tend to be lower, often $15 to $40. According to [WordStream's Local Service Ads analysis](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2023/07/26/local-service-ads), the overall range across home services spans roughly $6 to $80 depending on vertical and geography. Your actual cost depends on your bid, market competitiveness, and ranking factors.

**How long does it take to get verified and go live?**

Budget two to three weeks. Background checks through Evident take three to seven business days; license and insurance review adds another two to five. The most common delays are documentation mismatches and waiting for field employees to complete screening. Clean documentation and a single owner can move things under two weeks.

**Can I run Local Service Ads and standard Google Ads at the same time?**

Yes, and many contractors do. LSAs appear in a dedicated section separate from standard PPC ads, so your business can show up in multiple positions on the same results page. Budgets are set independently -- track performance from each channel separately so you know what each is actually costing you.

**What happens to my LSA ranking if I miss a lot of calls?**

It goes down. Google tracks your responsiveness rate and uses it as a ranking factor -- a low rate is one of the most reliable ways to undermine an otherwise well-set-up account. If you cannot consistently answer calls during business hours, address that before going live. Whether that means a part-time dispatcher, a booking service, or automated text-back for overflow, solve the coverage gap first.

**Are Local Service Ads worth it for a solo contractor or a very small operation?**

They can be, but the fit is tighter. A one-person operation needs to answer calls, handle estimates, complete jobs, and manage the business simultaneously -- consistent call coverage is harder when you are on a job all day. The economics can still work if you limit your ad hours to when you are actually available to answer and have an after-hours follow-up system for overflow. Many solo operators find LSAs work better after adding someone to handle scheduling, or after setting up [online scheduling](/blog/online-scheduling-for-contractors/) that lets customers book without a call.

**What if my category qualifies but my market is rural with low search volume?**

Low search volume is a real constraint. If your service area covers a small town or rural county, there may not be enough monthly searches to generate a steady LSA lead flow. In that case, the verification investment may not justify LSA as a primary channel. A well-optimized [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-contractor/) and organic presence tend to perform better in low-volume markets, where relationships and word-of-mouth carry more weight than paid placement.

**How do I increase my review count fast enough to compete in a new LSA market?**

Build review requests into your process systematically. After every completed job, send a text with a direct link to your Google review page -- two taps, no hunting. If you have a backlog of happy past customers, a personal outreach often produces results. Make review generation a consistent byproduct of good work, not a periodic campaign.
